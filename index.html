THE YEAR · 七十二候

Spring Summer Autumn Winter
S

tep outside before six and the world is lacquered in moisture. Each blade of grass carries its own cold bead, and where the sun strikes at an angle, whole fields seem dusted with frost. The air holds that first mineral chill of autumn, though afternoons still warm the skin.

Nature

Dragonflies rest motionless on fence posts, wings heavy with dew. Bush crickets have replaced cicadas as the dominant chorus, their calls threading through the darkness until dawn. In the paddies, rice stalks bow under ripening grain, their green fading to pale gold at the tips.

Table

. Fragrant rice cooked with sliced matsutake and light dashi, capturing the forest-floor aroma that defines early autumn luxury.

. Seared returning skipjack, fattier than spring's catch, served with grated ginger and ponzu to honor the season's shift.

. Sweetened chestnut paste, its golden color echoing the autumn fields as chestnuts reach their first harvest.

. Taro simmered in white miso, the tuber's sticky sweetness balanced by the fermented depth of aged paste.

Rite

This period often overlaps with Chōyō no Sekku, the Chrysanthemum Festival on the ninth day of the ninth month. In old practice, cloths were laid over chrysanthemum blossoms overnight to absorb their dew; by morning, people wiped their skin with these 'kikuzukin' to pray for longevity. Even today, chrysanthemum-shaped wagashi appear in shop windows, carrying forward the belief that autumn dew holds purifying power.
