MICRO-SEASON 41 · BEGINS JUL 28

Humid heat soaks the earth

土潤溽暑

Tsuchi uruōte mushiatsushi

THE YEAR · 七十二候

Spring Summer Autumn Winter
T

he air has thickened into something you can almost hold. Morning light arrives filtered through haze, and by noon the humidity has climbed the skin, settling into every fold of cloth. Even the shadows feel damp, and the cicadas' drone seems to vibrate through waterlogged air.

Nature

Higurashi cicadas begin their evening laments earlier each day, their plaintive calls piercing the heavy stillness. Lotus flowers open at dawn in temple ponds, their petals impossibly pristine above murky water. The soil in gardens remains dark even at midday, unable to dry between afternoon thunderstorms that arrive without warning and vanish just as quickly.

Table

. Miso soup with tiny freshwater clams, believed to restore the liver and replenish what the muggy heat depletes.

. Chilled eggplant slices dressed in dashi and ginger, their cool silkiness a balm against the oppressive humidity.

. Winter melon simmered with shrimp until translucent, its mild flesh absorbing the light broth like the earth absorbs this season's moisture.

. Loach simmered in broth, a Tokyo summer tradition said to build stamina when the humid heat saps all energy.

Rite

This is the heart of doyō, the eighteen days before autumn begins on the old calendar, when the soil itself is said to rest and one should not break ground. Many observe doyō no ushi no hi, eating unagi to fortify the body against the draining heat. The practice dates to the Edo period, when an herbalist allegedly advised an eel shop to market their wares as stamina food for the dog days.
