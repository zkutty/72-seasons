Cotton flowers open

THE YEAR · 七十二候

Spring Summer Autumn Winter
T

he morning air carries a different weight now — still warm, but with an undercurrent that whispers of change. Cotton fields across western Japan unfurl their bolls, each capsule cracking open to release clouds of white fiber that catch the slanting August light. In the garden, the tireless drone of cicadas has thinned, replaced by moments of startling quiet.

Nature

Dragonflies patrol the edges of rice paddies in greater numbers, their wings flashing copper and blue. The first susuki (pampas grass) plumes begin to emerge, still pale green but promising silver. Evening brings the tentative chirping of bell crickets testing their voices, while swallows gather on power lines, rehearsing the geometry of migration.

Table

. Tender kabocha squash simmered until soft and cloaked in a savory chicken mince sauce, perfect for the transitional appetite between summer's light fare and autumn's heartier cravings.

. Grated Japanese pear dressed with daikon oroshi, its cool sweetness and gentle texture offering refreshment as the season turns.

. Pacific saury formed into delicate fish balls and floated in a light dashi broth with ginger — the first taste of autumn's arrival in a bowl.

. Kyoho grapes folded into silken tofu dressed with sesame, a dish that bridges summer fruit with the subtle richness autumn begins to ask of us.

Rite

In the old cotton-growing regions of Nara and Osaka, this micro-season once marked the beginning of harvest preparations, with farmers watching for the precise moment when morning dew would not dampen the opening fibers. Cotton cultivation, introduced to Japan centuries ago, shaped entire village economies and textile traditions. Even now, small ceremonial cotton fields are maintained at certain shrines, where the opening of the first bolls is noted as a marker of seasonal passage.
