Thick fog descends

Micro-season 46 of 72 · Aug 23 – Aug 27

Cotton flowers open.

Wata no hana shibe hiraku

Cotton bolls split open to reveal white tufts, a small miracle of late summer when the heat begins its slow retreat.

The morning air carries a different weight now — still warm, but with an undercurrent that whispers of change. Cotton fields across western Japan unfurl their bolls, each capsule cracking open to release clouds of white fiber that catch the slanting August light. In the garden, the tireless drone of cicadas has thinned, replaced by moments of startling quiet.

Nature notes

Dragonflies patrol the edges of rice paddies in greater numbers, their wings flashing copper and blue. The first susuki (pampas grass) plumes begin to emerge, still pale green but promising silver. Evening brings the tentative chirping of bell crickets testing their voices, while swallows gather on power lines, rehearsing the geometry of migration.

Cultural note

In the old cotton-growing regions of Nara and Osaka, this micro-season once marked the beginning of harvest preparations, with farmers watching for the precise moment when morning dew would not dampen the opening fibers. Cotton cultivation, introduced to Japan centuries ago, shaped entire village economies and textile traditions. Even now, small ceremonial cotton fields are maintained at certain shrines, where the opening of the first bolls is noted as a marker of seasonal passage.
