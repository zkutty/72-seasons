Heaven and earth begin to cool

Micro-season 48 of 72 · Sep 2 – Sep 7

Rice ripens in the fields.

Kokumono sunawachi minoru

The rice paddies turn from green to gold as the grain bends heavy on the stalk, signaling the harvest to come.

A hush settles over the countryside as the rice heads bow under their own weight, the fields shifting from vivid green to pale gold in the softening September light. The air carries a faint sweetness — the scent of ripening grain mingling with late-summer grasses. Mornings arrive cooler now, mist pooling in the valleys before the sun burns it away.

Nature notes

Dragonflies patrol the paddy margins in great numbers, their wings catching the angled light. The chorus of cicadas has thinned, replaced by the first evening crickets testing their autumn songs. Herons stand motionless at field edges, hunting the small creatures displaced as water levels drop in preparation for harvest.

Cultural note

This period marks the anxious final days before rice harvest, when farmers watch the sky with particular attention. The tradition of kadomatsu no mizu — draining the paddies at precisely the right moment — requires reading subtle signs in the grain's color and the weather's mood. Many farming communities hold small pre-harvest prayers at local shrines, asking for continued fair weather and protection from the typhoons that threaten at this season.

稲穂垂れ風の重さを知る夕べ

inaho tare / kaze no omosa wo / shiru yūbe

Rice heads bowing — / this evening I feel / the weight of wind

In the fields, a single scarecrow watches over the golden sea, its sleeves stirring in a breeze that carries the first true breath of autumn.
