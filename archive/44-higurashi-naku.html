Evening cicadas sing.

Higurashi naku

The higurashi cicada's melancholy evening song marks autumn's true arrival, even as summer heat persists.

In the amber light of late afternoon, a singular cry rises from the forest edge — the higurashi cicada, whose voice carries the particular loneliness of seasons turning. The air still holds summer's weight, but this song changes everything. It is the sound of release, of something letting go.

Nature notes

The higurashi (Tanna japonensis) sings most intensely at dawn and dusk, its descending notes echoing through mountain valleys and temple groves. Dragonflies patrol the margins of rice paddies in greater numbers, their flight paths crisscrossing like calligraphy. Morning glory vines reach their peak bloom, unfurling new flowers each dawn that close by midday.

Cultural note

Obon observances reach their height during this micro-season, as families welcome ancestral spirits home with cucumber horses and eggplant oxen placed on household altars. The mukaebi (welcome fire) lit at dusk guides spirits back, while the higurashi's twilight song seems to bridge the worlds of the living and the departed. Many regions hold bon odori dances, their circular movements traced under paper lanterns.
