Micro-season 45 of 72 · Aug 17 – Aug 22

Thick fog descends.

Fukaki kiri matō

Dense morning fog blankets valleys and coastlines, softening the late-summer landscape as autumn's first breath mingles with lingering heat.

Dawn arrives veiled in white. The fog rolls in from the sea and rises from rice paddies, erasing the hard edges of mountains and dissolving distant villages into memory. The air holds both the coolness of what is coming and the humid weight of what remains — this is the hinge between seasons, felt most keenly in the hour before the sun burns through.

Nature notes

Cicadas still cry but their chorus thins, punctuated now by the first tentative calls of autumn insects at dusk. Dragonflies — red akatombo among them — patrol the edges of fields in greater numbers. The fog traps dew on spider webs, revealing their geometry in silver threads, while persimmon trees hold fruit that has only just begun to hint at orange.

Cultural note

This is the season of Obon's quiet aftermath. Families have sent their ancestors' spirits back to the other world with mukaebi fires, and a particular stillness settles over communities. In many regions, the fog itself is said to carry the departing souls, its veils a liminal curtain between worlds. Temple bells sound muffled in the thick air, and offerings of late-summer fruit remain on household altars a few days longer.

The fog lifts by mid-morning, but something of its softness lingers in the way light falls — gentler now, preparing us for what the maples already know.
