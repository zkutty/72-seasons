Micro-season 47 of 72 · Aug 28 – Sep 1

Heaven and earth begin to cool.

Tenchi hajimete samushi

The oppressive summer heat finally relents, and mornings carry the first unmistakable coolness of autumn's approach.

Before dawn, a new quality enters the air — not quite cold, but emptied of summer's thickness. The cicadas still cry, but their chorus has thinned, and in the pauses between their calls, you can hear the wind moving through bamboo with a cleaner, higher note. Step outside at first light and feel it: the earth itself seems to exhale after months of holding its breath.

Nature notes

Dragonflies patrol the rice paddies in greater numbers, their red and amber bodies catching the lower-angled sunlight. The susuki grass is beginning to send up its silver plumes at field edges and along riverbanks. Night-calling insects — suzumushi and matsumushi — grow louder as temperatures drop, their songs carrying farther in the denser evening air.

Cultural note

This period marks the traditional time for 虫聴き (mushi-kiki), the practice of listening to singing insects. Historically, people would capture suzumushi (bell crickets) in small bamboo cages to enjoy their clear, bell-like calls indoors. The custom reflects a particularly Japanese aesthetic sensibility — finding in the voices of insects not mere noise but a kind of natural music that announces autumn's melancholy beauty.
